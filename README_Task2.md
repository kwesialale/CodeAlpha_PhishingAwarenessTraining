# Phishing Awareness Training - CodeAlpha Task 2

This is my Task 2 project for the CodeAlpha Cybersecurity Internship. It's a training presentation aimed at people outside the cybersecurity field, covering how to recognize phishing emails and fake websites, the social engineering tactics attackers use, and how to respond if targeted.

## Objectives

This project was built to meet the goals of Task 2 (Phishing Awareness Training):

- Create a presentation focused on phishing attacks
- Explain how to recognize phishing emails and fake websites
- Educate on social engineering tactics used by attackers
- Provide best practices and tips to avoid falling victim
- Include real-world examples and an interactive quiz for engagement

## What's in this repo

- `Phishing_Awareness_Training_Verified.pptx` - the 17-slide training presentation
- `Written_Module.md` - the full written version of the training content
- `README.md` - this file

## Approach

Every statistic and case study in this training was checked against a real, named source before being included - the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3), the Anti-Phishing Working Group (APWG), Microsoft's Digital Defense Report 2025, INTERPOL, and NITDA (Nigeria's National Information Technology Development Agency). Sources are cited directly on the slides where each figure appears, and listed again in full on the References slide.

The training includes both a global real-world example (the $100 million Google/Facebook Business Email Compromise case) and Nigeria-specific examples (a 2016 INTERPOL-led BEC case and a current NITDA malware advisory), since the audience for this training isn't limited to one country or one level of technical background. Acronyms are written out in full on first use so the material is accessible to people who aren't already familiar with the field.

## Quiz

The deck includes a six-question interactive quiz covering phishing email recognition, social engineering terminology, Business Email Compromise, and incident response - each with an explained answer, not just a correct/incorrect label.

## Ethical note

All example phishing emails and scenarios in this training are fictional and clearly labeled as such. No real individual or organization is misrepresented.
